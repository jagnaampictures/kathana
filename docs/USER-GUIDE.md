## 1. Welcome to Kathana

Kathana is a creative writing and screenwriting application designed to help writers shape ideas, develop stories, and bring them to life.

Kathana Beta is built around a simple principle:

> **Download is public. Trial is public. Account is identity. Screenplays remain local.**

Kathana is designed as an **offline-first, local-first desktop application**.

Your screenplay projects are designed to remain on your computer rather than being stored in a Kathana cloud account.

---

# 2. Getting Kathana

Download the appropriate Kathana Beta release from the public Kathana distribution repository or the official Kathana website.

The current public Beta release provides:

**macOS — Apple Silicon (arm64)**

Additional platform builds may become available in later releases.

Always download releases from an official Kathana distribution channel.

---

# 3. Installing Kathana

After downloading Kathana:

1. Open the downloaded installer or disk image.
2. Install or copy Kathana according to the instructions for your operating system.
3. Launch Kathana.
4. Complete the initial welcome screen.

On macOS, the Beta is distributed as a standard desktop application.

Because the current Beta build is not distributed with a Developer ID Application signature and notarization, macOS may display additional security warnings when opening the application.

Only install Kathana from an official distribution source.

---

# 4. First Launch

When Kathana starts, the welcome screen provides the available access options.

Depending on the current Beta state, you may see:

- **Try Kathana — 14 days**
- **Sign in or create an account**

You do not need an account simply to download Kathana or begin the trial.

# 5. The 14-Day Trial

Kathana Beta provides a:

**14-day full-feature trial.**

The purpose of the trial is to let you experience the actual writing environment.

During the trial, the writing environment is not intentionally reduced to a demonstration mode.

You can use the supported writing, saving, preview, import, and export functionality available in the Beta.

### Trial information

The trial state is maintained locally on your computer.

Your screenplay content does not need to be uploaded to Kathana to use the trial.

The trial is associated with the local installation/device state rather than being a cloud screenplay account.

# 6. Creating and Writing a Screenplay

Use Kathana's editor to create and develop your screenplay.

The writing environment supports screenplay-oriented elements including:

- Scene headings
- Action
- Characters
- Dialogue
- Parentheticals
- Transitions
- Scene controls
- Omitted scenes
- Dual dialogue
- Title pages
- Headers and footers

Use the editor's formatting and scene controls to structure your screenplay as you write.

---

# 7. Writing in Kannada and Other Indian Scripts

Kathana Beta supports Kannada and multiple other Indian scripts.

The editor includes script-aware text handling intended for complex writing systems.

Kathana also uses grapheme-aware cursor and deletion behaviour so that characters composed from multiple Unicode elements can be handled more naturally.

This is particularly important when writing Kannada and other scripts where a visible character may consist of multiple underlying Unicode code points.

---

# 8. Saving Your Work

Kathana stores screenplay projects locally.

Use the application's save controls to save your work to the location you choose.

For important projects:

- Save regularly.
- Keep more than one backup.
- Avoid storing the only copy of an important screenplay on a failing or unreliable storage device.
- Keep important backups in a separate location where practical.

Kathana is not a replacement for your own backup strategy.

---

# 9. The `.kathana` File

Kathana's native screenplay project format is:

**`.kathana`**

A `.kathana` file contains the project information required by Kathana to work with the screenplay.

The format is intended to be opened and managed through Kathana.

Do not manually modify the internal contents of a `.kathana` file unless you understand the consequences.

Unsupported modification may result in:

- corruption;
- loss of project information;
- unexpected formatting;
- or incompatibility with Kathana.

Keep original backups of important `.kathana` files.

---

# 10. Opening Existing Projects

Open a `.kathana` project through Kathana's file-opening workflow.

Kathana may also support importing other supported formats.

When opening an existing project, verify the imported or opened document before continuing substantial work.

---

# 11. Multiple Windows

Kathana supports working with multiple document windows.

This can be useful when:

- comparing documents;
- referencing another screenplay;
- working between projects;
- or reviewing material while writing.

When working with multiple windows, make sure you know which document is active before saving.

---

# 12. DOCX Import

Kathana Beta supports DOCX import.

DOCX files created by other applications may contain formatting, structures, fonts, metadata, or features that do not have a direct equivalent in Kathana.

After importing:

1. Review the document.
2. Check scene structure.
3. Check character and dialogue formatting.
4. Check special characters and Indian-script text.
5. Check page layout and other important formatting.
6. Save the project as a `.kathana` file when ready.

Do not assume that every characteristic of the original DOCX will be reproduced exactly.

---

# 13. Exporting to Word

Kathana supports Word-compatible document export.

After exporting:

1. Open the exported document.
2. Review important pages.
3. Check formatting.
4. Check Indian-script text.
5. Check page breaks and layout.
6. Confirm that the document is suitable for its intended purpose.

Exported documents should always be reviewed before being sent as a final production or submission document.

---

# 14. Exporting to PDF

Kathana supports PDF export.

PDF is useful when you need a fixed-layout version of your screenplay.

After exporting, review:

- page breaks;
- scene formatting;
- dialogue;
- fonts;
- Indian-script rendering;
- headers and footers;
- title pages;
- and the overall page layout.

Keep the original `.kathana` project as your editable master.

---

# 15. Exporting to Fountain

Kathana supports Fountain export.

Fountain is a plain-text screenplay format.

Because plain-text formats have different capabilities from Kathana's native project format, some visual or application-specific features may not be represented in exactly the same way.

Review the exported Fountain file before using it as a final interchange file.

---

# 16. Preview

Kathana includes a screenplay preview environment.

Use preview to review how the screenplay is being interpreted and formatted.

Preview is particularly useful for checking:

- scene structure;
- dialogue;
- page flow;
- title pages;
- headers and footers;
- and general screenplay presentation.

Preview should be treated as a review tool, not as a substitute for checking the final exported document.

---

# 17. Search

Kathana includes screenplay search functionality.

Use Search to locate text within the current writing environment.

Search can be useful for finding:

- characters;
- dialogue;
- scenes;
- locations;
- repeated terms;
- or other screenplay material.

# 19. Light and Dark Themes

Kathana supports light and dark interface themes.

Choose the theme that best suits your writing environment.

# 20. Backups and Recovery

Kathana provides local backup and recovery-related functionality.

These mechanisms are intended to reduce the risk of losing work, but they should not be treated as a guarantee against every form of data loss.

For important writing:

### Recommended practice

Keep:

1. Your active `.kathana` project.
2. A separate backup copy.
3. An additional backup in another storage location where practical.

A backup that exists only on the same failing storage device may not protect you from hardware failure.

---

# 21. What Happens If You Lose Internet Access?

Kathana is designed as an offline-first application.

Many writing operations are performed locally.

However, certain account operations require an Internet connection, including functions such as:

- requesting an authentication code;
- verifying an account;
- checking account-related services;
- and other online entitlement operations.

The writing architecture does not depend on storing your screenplay in a cloud account.

---

# 22. Creating a Kathana Account

An account is used for identity and Beta access.

To create or access an account:

1. Choose **Sign in or create an account**.
2. Enter your email address.
3. Request the verification code.
4. Check your email.
5. Enter the 6-digit verification code.
6. Complete any required Beta or acquisition information.
7. Continue into Kathana.

Kathana does not require a traditional password for the Beta authentication flow.

Kathana uses a 6-digit email verification code.

The code is sent to the email address entered during authentication.

Kathana support will not need you to publicly post your verification code.

# 25. Your Account Does Not Contain Your Screenplay

This is an important part of Kathana's architecture.

Your Kathana account is used for:

- identity;
- authentication;
- Beta entitlement;
- account status;
- acquisition information;
- Terms acceptance;
- and related account services.

Your account is **not** a cloud screenplay library.

Signing in on another computer does not automatically download your local screenplay projects.

Your `.kathana` files remain on the storage locations where you saved them.

---

# 26. After the Trial Ends

When the 14-day trial ends, Kathana may require an eligible account sign-in to continue editing.

Kathana is designed so that trial expiry does not simply delete your local screenplay work.

Existing local projects can remain available for opening and exporting.

Editing may be restricted until you sign in with an eligible account.

You should therefore keep your `.kathana` files and backups.

---

# 27. Signing Out

If you sign out of Kathana, account-based access may change.

Kathana is designed to avoid unnecessarily interrupting an active writing session.

Depending on the application state, a sign-out or session-ending event may result in a notice while allowing the current writing session to remain usable until the appropriate state transition.

The exact behaviour may change in future Beta releases.

- Kathana version;
- operating system;
- what you were doing;
- what you expected to happen;
